## 1. Executive Summary

This forensic audit analyzes the biometric privacy and speaker-verification claims associated with the `FormantPreservingObfuscator` (pitch shifting and McAdams/VTLN spectral envelope warping).

**Primary Finding:**  
The repository provides valid proof of **acoustic signal perturbation** (pitch shifting and spectral envelope distortion) and demonstrates an empirical drop in x-vector neural embedding cosine similarity on 8 synthetic voice phrases. However, **this evidence does not establish speaker anonymization, de-identification, unlinkability, or protection against re-identification.**

---

## 2. Experimental Setup Analysis

### 2.1 Model Architecture & Purpose
- **Model Identified:** `anton-l/wav2vec2-base-superb-sv` (SUPERB benchmark speaker-verification model fine-tuned on VoxCeleb1).
- **Target Embedding:** Deep neural x-vector (512-dimensional embedding extracted from pre-classification layer).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Cosine similarity drop:
  $$\Delta_{\text{sim}} = 1.0 - \frac{\mathbf{e}_{\text{clean}} \cdot \mathbf{e}_{\text{obfuscated}}}{\|\mathbf{e}_{\text{clean}}\| \|\mathbf{e}_{\text{obfuscated}}\|}$$

### 2.2 Dataset & Trial Structure
- **Inputs:** 8 synthetic phrases synthesized via neural Edge-TTS (`en-US-GuyNeural`), recorded at 16 kHz.
- **Transformations:**
  - `none`: Semitone shift 0.0, alpha 1.0 (Cosine similarity = 1.0)
  - `mild`: Semitone shift 2.0, alpha 0.95
  - `medium`: Semitone shift 4.0, alpha 0.92
  - `strong`: Semitone shift 6.0, alpha 0.89
  - `strong_with_tempo`: Semitone shift 6.0, alpha 0.89, tempo rate 1.1

## 3. Detailed Audit Matrix

### 3.1 Demonstrated Observations (Supported by Evidence)
1. **Empirical F0 and Formant Shift:** The WORLD vocoder successfully shifts fundamental frequency ($F_0$) by the requested semitone values ($\pm 1.5$ jitter) and warps the spectral envelope via the McAdams parameter $\alpha$.
2. **Intelligibility Preservation:** Whisper STT maintains 0.0% WER across clean, mild, and medium conditions, with minor transcription errors ($WER \approx 0.167$) observed only at extreme strong warping.
3. **Neural Embedding Distance Perturbation:**
   - Under `mild` obfuscation: Mean cosine similarity is **0.976** ($\Delta = 0.024$).
   - Under `medium` obfuscation: Mean cosine similarity is **0.945** ($\Delta = 0.055$).
   - Under `strong` obfuscation: Mean cosine similarity is **0.914** ($\Delta = 0.086$).
   - Under `strong_with_tempo`: Mean cosine similarity is **0.877** ($\Delta = 0.123$).

### 3.2 Unsupported Claims (Must Be Corrected)
1. **Claim: "Voice Anonymization" or "De-identification Guarantee"**
   - **Status: UNSUPPORTED.** A cosine similarity of $0.87 - 0.91$ between clean and obfuscated embeddings remains exceptionally high in biometric speaker verification. Standard verification thresholds on VoxCeleb typically range between $0.60$ and $0.75$. At $0.87$, an automated speaker verification (ASV) system would still classify the obfuscated audio as originating from the same enrolled speaker with high probability.
2. **Claim: "Biometric Unlinkability Across Sessions"**
   - **Status: UNSUPPORTED.** No trial was conducted comparing two different utterances from the same speaker under randomized warp parameters to evaluate cross-session linkability.
3. **Claim: "Equal Error Rate (EER) or Biometric Protection Factor"**
   - **Status: UNSUPPORTED.** Zero different-speaker (impostor) trials exist in the benchmark. Without non-match scores, it is mathematically impossible to construct a DET (Detection Error Tradeoff) curve or compute EER, False Acceptance Rate (FAR), or False Rejection Rate (FRR).

## 4. Key Limitations of the Current Experiment

1. **Self-Comparison on Identical Linguistic Content:**  
   Every comparison evaluates phrase $i$ against transformed phrase $i$. Text-dependent similarity artificially elevates speaker embedding correlation compared to unconstrained text-independent verification.
2. **Single Synthetic Voice:**  
   All 8 test phrases were generated by a single synthetic neural voice (`en-US-GuyNeural`). No human voices, female speakers, or varied vocal tract geometries were tested.
3. **Absence of Impostor Distribution:**  
   Without measuring the distribution of cosine similarities between genuinely different speakers, the significance of a $0.086$ similarity drop cannot be interpreted.
4. **Vulnerability to Inversion / Acoustic Normalization:**  
   Because the pitch shift and spectral warp are deterministic linear/affine operations on $F_0$ and the spectral envelope, an adversary possessing knowledge of the obfuscator could readily normalize the signal back to its source distribution.

## 5. Required Protocol for Legitimate Biometric Privacy Evaluation

To support defensible claims of speaker de-identification, the following evaluation must be conducted:

1. **Multi-Speaker Dataset:**
   - Minimum 20 distinct human speakers (balanced male/female) from a standard corpus (e.g., LibriSpeech, VoxCeleb, or Common Voice).
   - Minimum 5 enrollment utterances and 5 test utterances per speaker.
2. **Standard Evaluation Trials:**
   - **Same-Speaker Clean vs. Clean:** Establishes baseline verification performance ($EER_{\text{clean}}$).
   - **Different-Speaker (Impostor) Trials:** Establishes the non-match score distribution.
   - **Same-Speaker Clean vs. Obfuscated:** Evaluates de-anonymization resistance. If the obfuscator is successful, the same-speaker obfuscated distribution should overlap with the different-speaker impostor distribution.
3. **Quantitative Metrics:**
   - Equal Error Rate (EER) before and after obfuscation.
   - Linkability metric ($\mathrm{D}_{\leftrightarrow}^{\text{sys}}$) defined in the VoicePrivacy Challenge evaluation protocol.
   - Zero-evidence threshold ($EER \rightarrow 50\%$).
